Percussion Documentation

Overview

The Percussion container generates struck percussion sounds using modal synthesis — a bank of damped sinusoidal oscillators (modes), all excited simultaneously by a mallet or hand strike at note onset. Each mode rings at its own frequency and decays at its own rate; their combined output produces the characteristic sound of a resonating body.

A single continuous parameter — Inharmonicity — interpolates between three acoustically grounded mode sets, covering the full range from a tuned drum skin to a metallic plate:

  • 0.0 — Harmonic series, near-integer overtones as produced by a tabla skin with the syahi tuning paste
  • 0.5 — Raw circular membrane, Bessel-function zero spacing as on an unweighted drum head
  • 1.0 — Inharmonic bell/gong spacing, metallic character

Because the note is treated as a trigger rather than a sustained event, the drawn note length on the score has no effect on the sound. The resonators ring naturally from the moment of the strike and decay according to the physical parameters regardless of how long the note is drawn.

Parameters

Parameter Type Default Range Description
strikePosition Float 0.5 0 – 1 Where on the membrane the strike lands. 0 = centre, 1 = rim. Centre emphasises low modes; rim excites all modes with more equal energy, producing a brighter, more complex tone.
strikeDuration Float 5.0 ms 0.5 – 50 ms Duration of mallet or hand contact. Short values produce a sharp click attack (hard stick, finger tip); longer values simulate a padded mallet or palm slap, with smoother onset and less high-frequency content.
inharmonicity Float 0.0 0 – 1 Blends between three mode-frequency sets. 0 = harmonic (tabla), 0.5 = raw circular membrane (Bessel), 1.0 = bell/gong. Values between anchors are continuously interpolated.
decayTime Float 1.0 s 0.005 – 15 s Decay time of the fundamental mode. Higher modes decay faster, scaled by highDecayRate. At 0.005 s even the fundamental is very short, giving a dry, damped transient.
highDecayRate Float 1.5 0.1 – 5.0 Controls how much faster high modes decay relative to the fundamental. Mode decay = decayTime × (f₀/fᵢ)^highDecayRate. Low values (0.3–0.5) give a metallic, all-modes-ring-equally character; high values (2–4) give a tabla-like tone where high modes die off almost instantly.
numModes Integer 12 2 – 16 Number of modal resonators computed per sample. More modes add spectral richness and computational cost. 8–12 is sufficient for most instruments; 16 is useful for dense metallic textures.
noiseGain Float 0.15 0 – 1 Amplitude of a short broadband noise burst at note onset, modelling skin texture, stick click, or hand-surface contact. The burst lasts 10 ms and decays exponentially. Set to 0 for a pure modal tone.
pitchMultiplier Float 1.0 0.25 – 4.0 Scales the fundamental frequency relative to the note pitch. 1.0 = note pitch as written, 2.0 = octave up, 0.5 = octave down.

How It Works

Modal Synthesis

At the moment a note begins, each of the numModes resonators is initialised with a frequency, an amplitude, and a per-sample decay factor, then excited by a mallet impulse. Each resonator is a second-order IIR filter of the form:

y[n] = cosCoeff × y[n-1]  −  r² × y[n-2]  +  excitation × modeAmplitude

where  cosCoeff = 2 · r · cos(ω)
       r        = exp(−1 / (decayTime_i × sampleRate))
       ω        = 2π × modeFrequency / sampleRate

The output is the sum of all resonators. Because the modes are at inharmonic (or near-harmonic) frequencies and at different phases, they produce natural-sounding beating and decay, not a static tone.

The membrane column uses the normalised zeros of the Bessel functions J₀, J₁, J₂, J₃. The bell/gong column is an empirically derived set that produces inharmonic metallic character while keeping all modes within a musically useful range at typical pitches.

Mallet Excitation

The excitation envelope is a half-cosine that decays from 1 to 0 over the strikeDuration window:

progress   = 1 − (samplesRemaining / strikeTotalSamples)
excitation = cos(progress × π/2)     // 1.0 → 0.0

This models the brief contact phase of a mallet or hand, where the energy transferred to the membrane decreases as the mallet rebounds. Short durations produce a sharp, high-frequency-rich attack; long durations smooth the onset and filter out the high modes, mimicking a soft mallet.

Notes Are Triggers

Unlike pitched instruments, percussive sounds have no "sustain" phase — the note length on the score has no effect on the sound. Internally, the audio engine caps the note body to 50 ms regardless of the written duration, then immediately enters tail mode. The modal resonators ring down naturally from the moment of the strike. This means:

  • A 1 ms note and a 1-bar note produce the exact same sound
  • Note length is used only to position the strike in time
  • Decay is controlled entirely by decayTime and highDecayRate

Usage Patterns

Basic Drum Hit

Percussion needs no upstream connections — it is a self-contained source:

[Percussion] → signalOut  (final output)

With IR Convolution (Recommended for Room)

Because the resonators decay cleanly to silence and no pre-tail fade is applied, the signal passed to the IR is unmodified and produces natural room reflections:

[Percussion] → signalOut → irIn → [IR Convolution] → signalOut

The IR convolution will ring through its full impulse response after the resonators have gone silent. This is the preferred chain for any pitched or metallic percussion — gong in a concert hall, bell in a cathedral, tabla in a wood room.

Layered Body and Ring

Two Percussion containers mixed with a Signal Mixer can layer a tight body tone with a longer resonant ring — useful for instruments like the dayan where the initial attack has a different character from the sustaining overtone:

[Percussion 1]  strikePos=0.6, decayTime=0.3, highDecayRate=3.0
    → signalA →  [Signal Mixer] → signalOut
[Percussion 2]  strikePos=0.3, decayTime=0.8, highDecayRate=1.2
    → signalB →  [Signal Mixer]

Expressive Modulation

Connecting an Envelope Engine to inharmonicity or strikePosition produces evolving timbre over the note's ring:

[Envelope Engine]  customEnvelope: 0.0 → 0.8 over noteProgress 0→1
    → envelopeValue → inharmonicity → [Percussion]
      (add, weight=0.8)

Result: strike begins as tabla, morphs toward gong character as it rings out

Pitched Harmonics

Using two Percussion containers at different pitch ratios and mixing them creates a rich layered texture useful for gamelan-like sounds:

[Percussion 1]  pitchMultiplier=1.0  → signalA
[Percussion 2]  pitchMultiplier=2.39 → signalB   (a minor 10th up)
Both → [Signal Mixer]

Design Notes

Decay Time vs Note Tail

Percussion does not need a Note Tail container. The resonators decay naturally to silence, which the audio engine detects automatically (silence below −60 dB for 100 ms stops tail rendering). Adding a Note Tail would shorten the ring artificially rather than extend it.

For very short decays (under ~50 ms) at low pitches, the fundamental mode may still be ringing when the silence threshold is reached. This is normal and produces the characteristic "thud" of heavily damped drums.

Modes and CPU

Kala pre-renders notes before playback, so CPU cost during playback is zero regardless of numModes. The only cost is render time, which is proportional to numModes × decayTime. For a gong with 16 modes and 10-second decay, the render takes slightly longer but plays back in real time.

Pitch and Inharmonicity

The note pitch on the score sets the fundamental mode frequency (mode 1). All other modes are multiples of this fundamental, scaled by the mode ratios for the current inharmonicity value. This means Percussion is a pitched instrument — playing it at different pitches on the score produces correctly tuned membrane resonances, as on a real tabla or timpani.

Troubleshooting

Sound Does Not Decay — Rings Indefinitely

Check that decayTime is not set extremely high (above 15 s) and that the silence threshold is being reached. The audio engine stops the tail after 30 seconds at the latest regardless of parameter values.

All Notes Sound the Same Pitch

Percussion uses the note's fundamental pitch to set mode frequencies. If all notes were drawn at the same pitch line on the score, they will sound the same. Draw notes at different pitch positions for tuned instrument behaviour (e.g., hand-tuned tabla playing).

Onset Too Noisy or Clicky

Lower noiseGain to reduce the broadband burst. Increase strikeDuration to soften the attack. If a sharp click persists at very short strike durations, it is the initial resonator excitation, which is physically accurate — raise strikeDuration above 3 ms to smooth it.

Gong / Bell Sounds Like a Drum

Ensure inharmonicity is above 0.7, highDecayRate is below 0.8 (so all modes ring for a long time), and decayTime is set to several seconds. Also increase numModes to 14–16 — the beating between closely-spaced inharmonic modes is what creates the metallic shimmer.

Tabla Sounds Too Metallic

Lower inharmonicity toward 0.0 (the syahi-tuned harmonic series), increase highDecayRate to 2.5–3.0 to damp the high modes quickly, and reduce numModes to 8–10. The characteristic tabla tone comes mostly from the first 4–5 harmonic modes.
